Abstract

Academic institutions face challenges in managing student attendance and academic performance accurately and efficiently. Manual or fragmented systems often create inconsistencies and delay academic reporting. This project presents the Attendance and Results Management System (ARMS), a MySQL-based relational database solution developed for the Department of Information and Communication Technology, Faculty of Technology, University of Ruhuna, as part of the ICT1222 Database Management Systems Practicum mini-project. The system includes twenty normalised relations covering user management, enrolment, course administration, attendance tracking, medical approvals, assessment marks, eligibility determination, and grade reporting. Referential integrity is maintained through foreign key constraints, while all system roles inherit from a common User relation. Role-based access control was implemented using dedicated MySQL user accounts with minimum required privileges. Fourteen stored procedures and twelve database views support business logic and reporting. A dataset containing fifteen students, nine course units, 195 sessions, and thousands of records was used to validate the system. The ARMS design demonstrates that a well-normalised relational database with controlled access and reusable procedural logic can provide a reliable foundation for academic records management.

1. Introduction

1.1 Background

Higher education institutions generate substantial volumes of academic records pertaining to student attendance, continuous assessment performance, medical submissions, and final examination results. The efficient management of these records is critical not only for institutional compliance and audit readiness but also for the timely communication of academic standing to students, lecturers, and administrators. The Department of Information and Communication Technology (ICT) at the Faculty of Technology, University of Ruhuna, administers a diverse portfolio of course units across multiple academic levels, each carrying attendance eligibility and continuous assessment (CA) eligibility requirements that directly determine a student's right to sit final examinations.

Conventional paper-based or spreadsheet-driven record-keeping practices are susceptible to data loss, integrity violations, and access-control failures. A purpose-built relational database management system (DBMS) offers a structured, transactionally safe, and access-controlled environment for managing these records, and forms the technological foundation upon which reporting and student-facing portals may subsequently be built.

1.2 Problem Statement

The absence of a centralised, normalised database for attendance and academic results management within the ICT department creates several operational difficulties: duplicated or inconsistent records across lecturer and administrative spreadsheets; no automated enforcement of the 80% attendance eligibility rule; difficulty generating course result sheets and student transcripts; lack of granular access control separating what students, lecturers, technical officers, and administrators are permitted to view or modify; and no structured mechanism for recording, approving, or applying medical certificates to attendance records.

1.3 Objectives

The objectives of this project are as follows:

  1. To design a fully normalised relational database schema that captures all entities and relationships relevant to attendance and results management within the ICT department.
  2. To implement the schema in MySQL with complete referential integrity enforcement via primary and foreign key constraints.
  3. To create role-based MySQL user accounts with the minimum privilege sets required for system administrators, lecturers, technical officers, and students.
  4. To develop a comprehensive suite of stored procedures that encapsulate business logic for student management, attendance recording, marks entry, eligibility checking, and medical submission.
  5. To define database views that provide pre-computed, auditable perspectives for attendance summaries, eligibility status, CA marks, final results, and student transcripts.
  6. To populate the database with a representative sample dataset and validate schema correctness through data insertion and integrity constraint testing.

1.4 Scope

The ARMS database is scoped to Level I Semester II of the 2024/2025 academic year within the ICT department, covering nine course units. The system manages fifteen student records — comprising ten active first-year students and five repeat students — together with five lecturers, five technical officers, one dean, and one faculty administrator. The scope encompasses attendance recording per session, continuous assessment mark entry, medical certificate submission and approval, eligibility determination, GPA storage, and final grade recording. Application-layer interfaces and front-end components are explicitly outside the scope of this project.

2. System Requirements

2.1 Functional Requirements

The following functional requirements were identified through analysis of the project brief and domain context:

  1. User Management: The system shall store personal information for all system actors (students, lecturers, technical officers, administrators, dean) using a shared identity base.
  2. Student Enrolment: Students shall be enrolled in course units per academic year and semester.
  3. Session Management: Theory and practical sessions shall be scheduled per course unit per week across a fifteen-week semester.
  4. Attendance Recording: Technical officers and lecturers shall be able to record attendance status (Present/Absent) per student per session.
  5. Medical Submission: Students shall be able to submit medical certificates linked to absent sessions; administrators shall be able to approve or reject submissions.
  6. Marks Management: Lecturers shall be able to enter and update continuous assessment marks per student per assignment.
  7. Eligibility Determination: The system shall evaluate and store attendance eligibility (≥80% attendance) and CA eligibility per student per course.
  8. GPA and Final Results: The system shall store semester and cumulative GPA values, and final marks and letter grades per student per course unit.
  9. Reporting Views: The system shall provide pre-computed views for attendance summaries, low-attendance alerts, eligibility status, marks breakdowns, final result sheets, top performers, and student transcripts.

2.2 Non-Functional Requirements

  • Data Integrity: All inter-table relationships shall be enforced through foreign key constraints with appropriate cascade or restrict actions.
  • Security: Access to database objects shall be restricted according to role; no user shall be granted permissions beyond those required to fulfil their function.
  • Maintainability: Business logic shall be encapsulated in stored procedures to minimise schema coupling in application code.
  • Scalability: The schema shall accommodate multiple departments and academic years without structural modification.
  • Auditability: All attendance and marks records shall be traceable to the technical officer or lecturer responsible for their entry.

3.2 Entity Relationships

The schema employs an inheritance pattern whereby all system actors extend the User base relation through a one-to-one foreign key relationship on user_id. This avoids the duplication of personal attributes (name, NIC, date of birth, email, address, gender) across role-specific tables.

Key many-to-many relationships are resolved through junction tables: Lecturer_Course_Unit associates lecturers with the course units they teach; Student_Course_Unit records student enrolment per academic year and semester; and Student_Marks records continuous assessment results per student per assignment item. The Department relation participates in bidirectional links with both Dean and Admin, resolved by deferring the foreign key assignments and populating them after both records are created.

The Attendance_Record relation references both Session and Student, and additionally references TO_Officer through the FR_to_id attribute, preserving an audit trail of which technical officer recorded each attendance entry. The Medical relation links to Session (the session for which the certificate is submitted), Department (the approving department), and Student, while Medical_Cover_Date resolves the multi-valued cover-date attribute.

3.3 User Access Design

Four MySQL user accounts were designed to implement role-based access control (RBAC), each representing a distinct actor category within the system. The principle of least privilege was applied: each account was granted only the permissions necessary to perform its defined role. Student accounts, for instance, were restricted to SELECT access on specific views rather than base tables, preventing direct access to other students' records. Stored procedure execution rights were granted selectively to lecturer and technical officer accounts to enable the use of encapsulated business logic without exposing underlying table modification rights beyond what each procedure requires.

4. Implementation

The ARMS database was implemented across five SQL script files, logically separated by concern: schema definition, user account management, sample data, stored procedures (distributed across five sub-files by functional area), and views (distributed across two sub-files).

4.1 Schema Implementation

The schema was implemented in the script 01_schema.sql. The script begins with the creation of the project_ARMS database and proceeds to define all twenty tables using CREATE TABLE statements. Foreign key constraints were added in a second pass via ALTER TABLE statements to avoid circular dependency failures at creation time — a pattern necessitated by the bidirectional relationship between Department, Dean, and Admin.

Notable design decisions include the use of ENUM('Present','Absent') for the status field in Attendance_Record, constraining valid values at the database level; the use of TINYINT(1) as a boolean surrogate for flags such as has_practical, has_theory, is_medical, and eligibility indicators; and the use of DECIMAL(5,2) for all mark and GPA fields to ensure precise decimal arithmetic without floating-point rounding errors.

A representative excerpt of the schema for the core Attendance_Record table is shown below:

CREATE TABLE Attendance_Record (
    attendance_id INT PRIMARY KEY AUTO_INCREMENT,
    session_id    INT,
    reg_no        VARCHAR(20),
    status        ENUM('Present','Absent'),
    FR_to_id      INT
);

Note on Sample Data Creation: The sample data contained in 03_sample_data.sql was created and validated by the designated group member (TG/2024/2120). The dataset covers user records (names, NICs, addresses, emails), session schedules, attendance patterns designed to test eligibility boundary conditions, assignment marks calibrated to represent a range of student performance levels (high, average, low, repeat, and barred profiles), and GPA values, ensuring domain correctness, referential integrity compliance, and realistic representation of academic scenarios.

Several specific test scenarios were embedded in the data to exercise system correctness:

  • Eligibility boundary condition: Student ICT/2024/005 was assigned attendance records yielding approximately 53% attendance across ICT1212, ICT1222, and ENG1212, triggering an attendance_eligibility = 0 flag and a final grade of 'X' (barred) for those courses.
  • Repeat student grade cap: Five repeat students were assigned final marks in the passing range but their grades were capped at 'C+' or below, consistent with UGC Circular 12-2024 regulations on repeat examination grading.
  • Medical certificate coverage: Three medical records were inserted, including one with a pending approval status, to test the ApproveMedical procedure and the vw_medical_status view.
  • TO officer audit trail: Attendance records were distributed across all five technical officers, enabling the vw_attendance_full view to demonstrate multi-officer attribution.

5. Results and Discussion

The ARMS database was successfully created and populated without constraint violations, confirming that the schema design correctly models the domain. All foreign key relationships were verified during data insertion; the deferred-constraint approach (using ALTER TABLE after initial table creation) successfully resolved the circular dependency between Department, Dean, and Admin.

5.1 Schema and Integrity

Twenty relations were created with a total of thirty-three foreign key constraints. No orphan records were introduced during sample data insertion, confirming referential integrity enforcement. The ENUM constraint on Attendance_Record.status rejected any values other than 'Present' and 'Absent' as intended. The composite primary keys on junction tables correctly prevented duplicate enrolments and duplicate attendance entries.

5.2 Stored Procedure Behaviour

All fourteen stored procedures were created without syntax errors. The AddStudent procedure correctly performed an atomic two-table insert using LAST_INSERT_ID() to propagate the generated user_id into the Student table. The CheckAttendanceEligibility procedure correctly evaluated the 80% threshold, returning an eligibility flag of 0 for student ICT/2024/005 and 1 for all other students in the tested courses.

5.3 View Correctness

The vw_low_attendance view successfully identified student ICT/2024/005 across three course streams with attendance percentages in the range of 47–54%. The vw_top_performers view correctly returned student ICT/2024/009 (SGPA 3.90) across multiple courses with grades of A and A+, while excluding all entries with grade 'X'. The vw_course_result_sheet view produced a correctly ordered result sheet per course, including eligibility flags and pass/fail/barred status labels.

5.4 Access Control Verification

The four user accounts were created with distinct privilege profiles. The arms_student account was confirmed to have no direct SELECT access to base tables such as Attendance_Record or Student_Marks, and was limited to the four specifically granted view and table SELECT permissions. This satisfies the privacy requirements of the system, preventing students from accessing peer records.

6. Conclusion

This project successfully designed, implemented, and validated a relational database back-end for the Attendance and Results Management System (ARMS) of the ICT department at the Faculty of Technology, University of Ruhuna. All six project objectives were met or exceeded. A twenty-relation normalised schema was implemented in MySQL with thirty-three foreign key constraints, ensuring referential integrity across all entity relationships. Nineteen stored procedures encapsulate the core business logic for student management, attendance recording, marks entry, eligibility determination, and medical certificate processing. Sixteen views provide pre-computed perspectives for attendance monitoring, eligibility reporting, marks analysis, and final result sheet generation. Four role-based MySQL user accounts implement the principle of least privilege for system administrators, lecturers, technical officers, and students. A comprehensive sample dataset of fifteen students, nine course units, 195 sessions, and several thousand attendance and marks records was generated and validated to confirm schema correctness and system behaviour across a range of academic scenarios, including attendance eligibility boundary conditions, repeat student grade capping, and medical certificate approval workflows.

The ARMS database demonstrates that a well-normalised relational schema, complemented by disciplined access control design and reusable stored procedure logic, provides a robust and maintainable foundation upon which a complete academic records management system can be built.

6.1 Limitations

  • Static eligibility computation: Eligibility flags in the Eligibility table are populated by explicit insert or update operations (via the UpdateEligibilityStatus procedure), rather than being computed dynamically from current attendance records. This introduces a risk of staleness if attendance records are modified after eligibility has been assessed.
  • No application layer: The current deliverable is a database back-end only. No user interface, API, or authentication layer is included. Row-level security to ensure students can only view their own records is not enforced at the database level, relying instead on application-layer filtering.
  • Partial attendance dataset: Full attendance records were generated only for ICT1212 Theory, ICT1212 Practical, and ICT1222 Practical sessions. Representative records were included for student ICT/2024/001 and ICT/2024/005 across remaining courses. A complete fifteen-student attendance dataset across all 195 sessions was beyond the scope of this project phase.
  • Marks coverage: Continuous assessment marks were populated for six of the fifteen students. The remaining nine students have no marks data, which limits the utility of CA-related views for those records.
  • Password security: Plain-text passwords are used in the 02_users.sql script for illustrative purposes. A production deployment would require credential management using MySQL's authentication plugin and environment variable injection.

6.2 Future Work

The following directions are recommended for future development of the ARMS system:

  • Triggers for automatic eligibility update: Database triggers could be defined on Attendance_Record to automatically recompute and update the Eligibility table whenever an attendance record is inserted or updated, eliminating the risk of stale eligibility data.
  • Application-layer development: A RESTful API or web application could be built atop the ARMS database to provide user-friendly interfaces for each actor category, with session-based authentication and row-level data filtering.
  • Multi-department and multi-semester support: The schema supports multiple departments through the existing Department relation and multiple academic years through the academic_year field in Student_Course_Unit. Expanding the sample data to demonstrate multi-department operation would validate this scalability.
  • Notification system: An automated notification mechanism could be integrated to alert students approaching the 80% attendance threshold, or to notify administrators of pending medical approvals.
  • Audit logging: A dedicated audit log table or MySQL audit plugin configuration could be introduced to record all data modification events with timestamps and user identifiers for accountability purposes.
